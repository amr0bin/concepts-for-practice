What this system asks of you
Prioritisation is constant. With one charger and six batteries draining at different rates, you are always mid-decision — even when you appear to be resting.
Neglect compounds. A battery that runs flat doesn't just mean one lever fails — it raises the drain rate on others, and can take days to recover.
The ceiling is real, not a feeling. Pushing past 80% doesn't unlock more capacity — it accelerates drain across all batteries simultaneously.
On good days, things can feel normal. They are not — you are experiencing the result of careful management. That management is invisible work.
On catastrophe, acceptance, and the 80%
⚠️

Catastrophic to ignore

Pretending the 80% ceiling doesn't exist — pushing as if capacity is still 100% — doesn't create more output. It collapses the system. Batteries drain to zero. Recovery takes longer. The ceiling isn't cruel; ignoring it is.

💙

Difficult to accept

80% of a person's full capacity is still an enormous amount of living. But the loss is real (including secondary losses like career milestones, friendships, financial stability, and more), and grief for the lost 20% is not self-pity — it is honest. Acceptance doesn't mean forgetting what was. It means building something true from what is.

✨

The invisible expertise

Managing six batteries with one charger, across shifting conditions, under a ceiling others can't see — this is sophisticated, exhausting, ongoing work. It rarely gets recognised. But it is what makes everything else possible.

🌱

What good management looks like

Sleep protected. Food prioritised. Movement measured, not maximised. People chosen carefully. Meaning tended. Stress met with tools, not willpower. None of these are luxuries. They are the maintenance schedule of a changed but still running system.
